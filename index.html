About

Two signs, one picture

Fluctuating asymmetry

Small random differences between the left and right halves of a leaf. They build up over a season, so a high value points to long-term stress.

Chlorophyll index

Calculated from the green and red channels of the photo. Paler, yellower leaves score lower, which shows how the plant is doing right now.

Two-layer map

The demo map shows both indices as separate layers, so you can compare long-term and current stress at the same spot.

        Method

        How the numbers are made

        Fluctuating asymmetry compares the left and right halves of a leaf. In the classic protocol you measure veins and angles by hand. This demo measures the leaf outline instead.

        What the page does

        1. Separates the leaf from the background by colour and keeps the largest connected shape.
        2. Draws a symmetry axis from the leaf base to its tip.
        3. Measures the distance from the axis to the edge on each side at 20, 35, 50, 65 and 80 percent of the leaf length.
        4. Averages |L − R| / (L + R) over the five positions to get the FA index.

        Chlorophyll index

        The average over leaf pixels, after scaling the colours so the white background reads as white:

        ChlI = (G − R) / (G + R)

        Similar in spirit to NDVI, but built from a normal photo. Range −1 to +1.

        Stress index

        Stress = (FAnorm + (1 − ChlInorm)) / 2

        0 means no sign of stress, 1 means critical. FA is scaled from 0.030 to 0.065 and ChlI from −0.15 to 0.25.

        Limits. This is a teaching demo. Outline measurements are rougher than vein measurements, and photo angle, leaf curl and lighting all change the result. Use it to compare leaves, not as a certified measurement.
